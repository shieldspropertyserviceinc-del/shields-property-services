TRANSCONA NEIGHBOURHOODS.

Where we work, street by street — and what each pocket means for snow clearing.

Transcona is a working-class neighbourhood of families and long-time residents, living mostly in bungalows with medium-sized front and back lawns. Home prices run slightly below the city average. The original core was built from the 1940s through the 1960s — post-war bungalows on fifty-foot lots — with 1970s and 80s split-levels and newer builds toward the edges. Here is what that means, pocket by pocket.

Canterbury Park

East-side family pocket mixing detached and attached single-family homes with townhouses — older resale homes alongside newer construction filling in. Double garages are common on the newer builds.

For snow clearing: mix of single and double driveways; townhouse rows need coordinated clearing so one drifted unit does not block the next.

Kildare

Established west-Transcona streets (Kildare Avenue area) of classic bungalows, many with detached garages and fenced yards — the heart of post-war Transcona.

For snow clearing: standard single driveways; mature trees mean more fall debris to clear before the snow flies.

The Meadows

Newer-feeling pocket with side-by-side homes and infill development — a sought-after area for first-time buyers, with tidy, compact lots.

For snow clearing: narrower lots and shared driveways in places; efficient route planning matters here.

Griffin

One of Transcona's named neighbourhood pockets within the broader bungalow fabric — settled streets, long-time residents.

For snow clearing: typical Transcona single driveways; many long-term owners who value reliability over price.

Mission Gardens

Newer residential neighbourhood on the greater Transcona edge, near the Transcona Golf Club — larger, more modern homes than the post-war core.

For snow clearing: bigger driveways and longer walkways; the kind of property where a per-visit plan adds up fast and a seasonal plan pays off.

Park Manor

Residential pocket within the Transcona area — part of the same bungalow-and-family fabric as the surrounding streets.

For snow clearing: standard residential driveways; close to our core routes for fast storm-day service.

Regent

The bustling commercial corridor — Regent Avenue shops, restaurants, and the Club Regent Casino — with residential streets branching off it, including newer builds toward the Regent side.

For snow clearing: mix of older bungalows and newer homes; also home to small businesses that may need storefront walkway clearing — ask us for a quote.

Transcona South

Growing area blending older well-kept homes with new development — tree-lined streets, plus townhouses, duplexes, and low-rise apartments in the city's secondary plan for the area.

For snow clearing: the widest mix of property types in Transcona; newer developments may have condo-managed common areas alongside private driveways.

ONE NOTE ON DRAINAGE.

Parts of Transcona sit on low, clay-heavy ground — real estate listings here routinely flag sump pumps and drainage as things to check. For snow clearing, that means spring melt pooling where winter snow was piled. We pile snow with melt in mind, not just wherever is closest.
